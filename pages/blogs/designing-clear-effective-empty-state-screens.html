Designing Clear and Effective Empty State Screens

empty state designproduct UXonboardinginterface design
Date: 05 Nov 26

By Webx Design Studio•0 views

An empty state is never just an empty screen. It is a moment when a person needs orientation: Where am I? What happened? What can I do next?

Handled well, this small moment makes a product feel calm, considerate and easy to learn. Handled poorly, it can feel like a dead end. This guide shows how to design empty states that turn absence into a useful next step.

Why Empty States Matter More Than They Seem

First-time users meet empty states before they see the product at its most valuable. Returning users meet them after clearing a filter, completing work, changing permissions or searching for something that does not exist. In each case, the product has a chance to explain itself.

A blank panel leaves people to infer the system model on their own. A purposeful empty state reduces that cognitive load. It tells people what this place is for, why it is empty right now and how to make progress without opening a support ticket.

Make the moment legibleSay what the person is seeing in language they would use, not in internal system terminology.
Protect momentumOffer a route forward before uncertainty becomes hesitation or abandonment.
Teach just enoughReveal a helpful capability in context instead of forcing people through an explanation elsewhere.

Know Which Empty Moment You Are Designing

Not all empty states ask for the same response. Start by naming the cause. This helps the team avoid pasting the same friendly illustration and button into every blank area.

First useOrientation and confidenceExplain the value of this area and invite the first meaningful action.
No resultsRecovery from a failed search or filterRepeat the query, suggest changes and offer a clear way to reset.
CompletedClosure and continuityAcknowledge progress and point to the most relevant next task.
Temporarily unavailableHonesty and reassuranceState the issue, preserve trust and say when or how to try again.
Permission or eligibility gapContext and agencyExplain what access is needed and provide a route to request it.

The Anatomy of a Clear Empty State

The strongest states are concise, not sparse. They use a small set of elements in a deliberate order so someone can scan the situation and decide what to do.

  1. A specific headline: Name the state plainly, such as “No projects yet” or “No matches for ‘invoice’.” Avoid generic labels like “Nothing here.”
  2. A helpful explanation: Give only the missing context: why the area is empty, what normally appears here, or what changed.
  3. A single primary action: Make the main next step easy to identify and phrase it as a verb people understand.
  4. Optional secondary support: Add a subtle reset, template, import path or help link only when it solves a realistic alternative need.
A good empty state does not decorate an absence. It makes the absence understandable and gives the person back a sense of control.

Choose the Right Next Action

The primary action should reflect the smallest meaningful step to change the state. “Create project” is useful when a person is starting fresh; “Clear filters” is useful after a narrow search; “Request access” is useful when permission—not content—is the blocker.

Avoid using the empty state to force a product tour, a marketing upgrade or a broad dashboard detour. Those choices may serve the business, but they do not necessarily help the person solve the immediate problem. Relevance earns trust.

Quick action test

  1. Can the person complete the action without leaving the task they came to do?
  2. Will it create real value or simply add another setup step?
  3. Is the action still understandable without the surrounding page?
  4. If the person cannot take it, is there a clear, quieter alternative?

Use Visuals and Tone with Purpose

An illustration can make a blank state feel intentional and warm, but it should support comprehension rather than compete with it. Use visuals to reinforce the action or the product metaphor: a box waiting to be filled, a route ready to begin, a workspace ready for a first project.

Keep the voice human and direct. Humour can be memorable, but it should never dismiss a frustrating situation such as a failed search, lost access or system outage. The more serious the interruption, the more the interface should focus on clarity, status and recovery.

Design for Difficult States Too

Teams often design the cheerful first-use state and overlook the emptiness created by filters, errors and permissions. These states deserve equal care because they are often where confidence is most fragile.

  • No search results: preserve the query, explain what was searched and offer a reset or broader alternative.
  • Filters removed everything: show the active filters and let people clear or adjust them in place.
  • Loading or delay: distinguish a temporary wait from a genuinely empty collection; never make users guess.
  • Access restricted: explain the rule without exposing sensitive information, then offer the appropriate request path.
  • Deleted or completed work: acknowledge what happened, make recovery possible when it is safe and suggest the next useful destination.

A Repeatable Empty-State Framework

Use this four-part sequence during design critique and content review. It keeps the conversation focused on the person’s situation, not just on whether the page looks finished.

1. DiagnoseWhat caused this state, and what does the person believe they were trying to do?
2. ExplainWhat is the smallest truthful message that makes the system’s status clear?
3. InviteWhat is the most relevant, achievable next action from this exact moment?

Then test the state with real content, small screens, keyboard navigation, long translations and different permission levels. An empty state that works only in a perfect mockup is not ready for the product.

Pre-Launch Checklist

Effective empty-state checklist

  1. The headline says exactly what is empty or unavailable.
  2. The explanation matches the true cause of the state.
  3. The primary action is relevant, visible and phrased as a clear verb.
  4. Secondary routes are present only when they help someone recover.
  5. Images and icons do not carry meaning that is missing from the text.
  6. Keyboard and assistive-technology users can understand and complete the next step.
  7. The state has been checked for first use, no results, loading, permissions and recovery.
  8. Analytics distinguish between seeing the state, taking action and leaving it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an empty state and an error state?

An empty state means there is currently no content to show; an error state means the product could not complete an expected action. They can look related, but the message and recovery action must reflect the real condition.

Do empty states need illustrations?

No. A visual is optional. Use one when it strengthens orientation or tone, and skip it when concise text, status information or a form of recovery deserves the focus.

How do we measure whether an empty state is working?

Watch whether people take the intended next action, recover from search or filter states, and complete the associated task. Pair that behaviour with usability testing so you know whether the message itself is clear.

Make the Blank Moment Feel Like Progress

Empty states are small pieces of product writing, interaction design and brand behaviour working together. When they explain the moment honestly and offer the next useful step, a blank area becomes a quiet demonstration of product quality.
